🧰 SMD board testing workflow

1. Compare before replacing

Use a known-good board of the same version as your reference. Photograph both boards and note component markings and locations. Check for corrosion, cracked parts, solder bridges and damaged pads.

2. Power OFF: inspect and check for shorts

Remove batteries or external power and safely discharge capacitors. Use continuity only to check obvious connections, fuses and tracks. A beep between a supply rail and ground does not automatically mean a short.

3. Diode mode: compare semiconductor junctions

Use diode mode on unpowered boards. Test the same component at the same pads, in both probe directions, on the good and suspect boards. Record both readings. In-circuit paths can affect readings; isolate a leg only if you are trained and it is necessary.

4. Resistance: compare like for like

Measure the same resistor or rail on both boards using the same meter range and probe points. Capacitors may make the reading change as they charge. Low resistance alone is not proof of a fault.

5. Powered checks: only known test points

Use DC volts, black lead in COM and red lead in V/Ω. Measure only identified points with the correct normal supply connected. Compare the same startup stage on both boards. Never use resistance, continuity or diode mode on a powered board, and never put the meter in current mode directly across the battery.

6. Keep a comparison log

Record component location/marking, meter mode, probe direction, good-board reading, suspect-board reading and the test condition. A difference is a clue to investigate—not proof the component is bad.

🔬 SMD component test cards

SMD resistor

Meter: Ω resistance · Power: disconnected.

Probe: one probe on each end cap of the resistor. Compare the same part on the known-good board.

Expected: near its marked value, but parallel paths can make an in-circuit reading lower. Tiny SMD codes can be ambiguous; confirm the code before deciding the value.

If different: inspect for cracks/burn marks and compare surrounding circuitry. To confirm, one end may need lifting by someone competent with SMD rework.

SMD ceramic capacitor

Meter: Ω resistance or diode mode for comparison · Power: disconnected and capacitor safely discharged.

Probe: one probe on each end. Compare both boards at the same capacitor and in the same mode.

Expected: readings depend heavily on the circuit; a low reading may be from other components on the rail. Resistance may change as capacitors charge from the meter.

If different: compare the whole rail to ground on both boards. A multimeter alone may not prove a ceramic capacitor is faulty; isolating it or using suitable capacitance/ESR equipment may be needed.

SMD diode

Meter: diode mode · Power: disconnected.

Probe: measure across the two ends, then swap probes and record both readings. Use the board markings or datasheet to identify the cathode when possible.

Expected: many silicon diodes show a forward drop roughly 0.5–0.8 V and OL in reverse; Schottky and other types differ. In-circuit paths may change both readings.

If different: near 0 V in both directions can suggest a short; OL both ways can suggest an open device. Confirm out of circuit if needed before replacing.

SOT-23 transistor / MOSFET

Meter: diode mode · Power: disconnected; discharge capacitors first.

Probe: first identify the exact part marking and datasheet pinout. Do not assume which pin is gate, drain, source, base, collector or emitter from package shape. Test matching pins in both directions on each board.

Expected: depends on device type and internal protection/body diode. There is no universal good reading for every three-pin SMD transistor.

If different: note each pair of pins and both polarities. A near-short across a MOSFET's drain-source in both directions may indicate damage, but other board paths can mimic it.

SMD fuse / zero-ohm link

Meter: continuity or low Ω · Power: disconnected.

Probe: one probe on each end. Check whether the board marking identifies it as a fuse or 0 Ω link.

Expected: normally very low resistance. OL can mean an open fuse/link, but first confirm the component's purpose.

If open: find the reason it failed before replacing. Never bridge a fuse with wire or fit a higher rating.

Voltage regulator / IC

Meter: DC volts for powered checks; Ω/diode only with power off.

Probe: identify ground, input, output and enable pins from the exact datasheet or schematic before measuring. Black probe to confirmed ground; red probe to the known test point.

Expected: compare the same pins at the same startup stage on a known-good board. Output depends on input, enable state and load.

If different: check input and enable before assuming the IC is faulty. Do not inject voltage into an unknown rail.
